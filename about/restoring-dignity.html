Restoring dignity

Dignity is at the heart of our work. For a child in treatment, dignity can quietly erode: routines are set by others, the body is examined and discussed, play and school disappear, and the child becomes “the patient in bed 4.” Our programmes are designed to give that dignity back.

Six ways we protect dignity

Seen as a child

Every session begins with a child's name, interests and ideas, not their condition.

Choice and agency

Where most decisions are made for them, being asked what they would like to do matters.

Valued as a contributor

Children create stories, drawings and art that are theirs, and are celebrated for what they make.

Belonging

Group activities and peer mentorship tell children they are part of a community.

Hope and a future

Through creative expression and mentorship, children imagine and plan beyond treatment.

Privacy and voice

Children's stories are shared only with care, consent and their own say in how they are told.

“When a child laughs, chooses, creates or makes a friend in a hospital ward, they are reminded that they are a whole person.”
