An interrupted childhood

When a child is diagnosed with a chronic illness, the first priority is medical. But illness affects much more than the body.

What children can lose

More than health

Time in hospital

Long periods away from home and routine.

School and learning

Missing school and falling behind, sometimes repeating grades.

Friends and classmates

Being separated from the people they play with.

Play

Fewer chances to take part in ordinary childhood activities.

Belonging

Stigma and feeling different from their peers.

Routines and experiences

Losing important routines and social experiences.

Confidence

Fewer chances to explore interests and build self-belief.

Identity

Growing up with an identity shaped around being a patient.

The child is still growing, learning and developing, but many of the experiences that support that development have been disrupted. We call this an interrupted childhood.

Psychosocial challenges

How it feels

These disruptions affect how children feel about themselves. Children may experience anxiety around treatment, loneliness, low self-esteem and a sense of being defined by their diagnosis.

Adolescents may face added pressure around identity, their future and stigma among peers, and families carry stress that children often sense.

In busy health settings these needs are rarely the first priority, and they often go unaddressed.
